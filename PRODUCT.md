# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

School principals, administrators, and finance staff at Indian private and government-aided schools (K-12). They are in their 30s-50s, comfortable with smartphones but not developer tools, deal with 200-2000 students per school. They use the site from a desktop in their school office or on a phone while moving between classrooms. Their job: evaluate whether EduFee is worth switching to, then get a demo or sign up.

Secondary: parents researching the school's systems.

## Product Purpose

EduFee is a four-app school management platform built specifically for Indian schools. It replaces manual fee ledgers, WhatsApp chains, and disconnected software with one clean system: fee collection with receipt PDFs, attendance, parent communication, and event management. Success means a school admin understands all four apps, sees the pricing, and either books a demo or signs up in under 3 minutes.

## Brand Personality

Simple, calm, focused. The interface should feel like a well-organised school office — not cold corporate, not playful startup. Confident clarity over decoration. Built for India, not transplanted from Silicon Valley.

## Anti-references

- Generic US SaaS aesthetic (Notion, Linear, Vercel): minimal dark mode, purple/indigo gradients, monospace type, developer-cultural signals. Wrong audience.
- Old Indian government software: dense tables, clashing reds and greens, no breathing room. Association to avoid.
- Aggressive startup landing pages with countdown timers, excessive badge stacking, and manipulative urgency copy.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the tool, not the promise.** Every section should ground claims in a specific screen, flow, or number — not adjectives. "We generate PDF receipts instantly" with a receipt visible beats "streamlined billing experience."
2. **India-specific, not generic.** Rupee symbols, Indian class naming (Class 1-10), school year (April-March), regional language signals. The page should feel like it was built here.
3. **Calm confidence.** No shouting. Typography does the heavy lifting; decoration does not. White space is not wasted space.
4. **Simplicity that earns trust.** Schools are risk-averse. The visual language should say "reliable, already used by others like you" not "innovative disruption."
5. **Four apps, one price.** The pricing message is a differentiator — competitors charge per module. Every section can plant this seed.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. The audience includes older administrators on lower-resolution screens, so body text must be ≥16px and contrast must be checked at every step. Reduced-motion alternatives for all scroll animations. No color-only information encoding.
